## Decision: mirrors (5 lines)

- Manual-first. A mirror is a polygon picked on the wall in the Studio (≥ 3 corners), stored in `mirrors.json` beside `pois.json`. Applying it is an explicit button; no mirror means no change to any file.
- The automatic detector only suggests. Geometry behind a wall plane could be a real window, door or alcove, so it is never cut without a human. This costs one click per mirror and removes the false-positive risk.
- One cut, used everywhere. The mesh is cut and capped, the nav points are filtered, the mirror is added as a wall, the splat masks come from the same polygon, and behind-mirror splats are culled. Every synthesized surface is flagged inferred, with `cap: false` to switch the caps off.
- Mirror support is wired for imported scans (Route A, the primary route). For photo runs (Route B), mirrors are stored but not applied. This is reported, not hidden.
- Not built: inferring what the wall behind the mirror looks like. The cap is a flat surface coloured from the wall around the mirror, labelled inferred.

## Block-wise splat training (built 17 Nov, second pass; off by default)

Block training (split the floor into blocks, train each to small-room density, merge) is the real fix for B1. It changes the trainer's structure, the alignment step, the export and the time budget, and it cannot be checked without many GPU runs. Under the "no laptop testing, Round 2 on 30 Nov" constraints, this branch makes the density problem measurable (splats/m² per block in the health report) and adds the safe, flag-guarded parts. Block training is the first item after Round 2.
