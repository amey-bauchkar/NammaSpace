Capture SOP – photographing a room for Namma Space

Who this is for: anyone with a smartphone. No measuring tape, markers or 3D skills are needed. Time: about 10–15 minutes for one room. Output: 60–150 photos, or one 1–3 minute video.

Status of this SOP: it is derived from the reconstruction method (structure from motion + multi-view stereo) and from Phase 12–13 measurements on public datasets. It has not yet been validated with the team's own iPhone 12 capture (pending validation item Q-T-11). The app's capture check enforces the limits written here.

1. Before you start (1 minute)

Do Why
Switch on all lights; open curtains only if the windows are not in the frame Dark photos are noisy and blurry; bright windows blind the camera
Use the main (1×) camera; do not zoom; keep the same phone for the whole room Each lens / zoom needs its own calibration
iPhone: Settings › Camera › Formats › Most Compatible The app accepts JPEG / PNG; HEIC photos are refused with a message
iPhone video: Settings › Camera › Record Video › turn HDR Video off HDR video loses its colours when converted
Clean the lens A smudge blurs every photo
Move small things that will move (pets, people) out of the room Moving objects cannot be reconstructed

2. Photos (recommended)

  1. Start at a corner. Stand about one step in from the wall and face into the room.
  2. Walk along the walls. Take a photo, take one small step (about 0.5 m) sideways, take the next photo. Consecutive photos should share about two thirds of what they show.
  3. Tilt slightly down so that the floor is in the lower third of every photo. Floors are needed for walking and navigation; Phase 12–13 measurements show floors are the most often missing part.
  4. Hold still for each shot (sharp beats many). In low light, lean on something.
  5. Go all the way around, then do a second, lower lap (waist height) around large furniture (tables, sofas, beds) so their legs and the floor around them are seen.
  6. Doorways and corridors: take 3–4 photos while walking through each doorway so the rooms connect.
  7. Plain surfaces (white walls, glass, mirrors, shiny tiles) cannot be reconstructed on their own: always include an edge, a picture, furniture or a corner in the frame.
  8. Typical counts: small room 40–80 photos, living room 80–150. The app accepts 10–300 per project.

3. Video (alternative)

  • One continuous take, 1–3 minutes, walking slowly (about 30 cm per second) along the walls with the same rules (face into the room, floor visible, no fast turns).
  • Turn in place slowly; never whip the phone around.
  • The app extracts up to 200 sharp frames automatically (2 per second, sharpest of each group).
  • One video per project; do not mix photos and video in the same project.

4. Upload and check (in the Studio)

  1. Create a space, drag the photos or the video into the upload area.
  2. Press Check my capture. It reports, before any long processing:
  3. blocking problems (too few photos, unreadable files, HEIC, mixed photos + video);
  4. warnings: blurry, too dark / too bright, few details, near-duplicates, photos that overlap nothing, groups of photos that do not connect.
  5. Retake the flagged photos if the check suggests it, then press Start reconstruction.

5. Optional: real-world size

Photos alone do not tell the true size of a room. The model is always shown correctly shaped; to show distances in metres, open the 3D walkthrough, choose Measure / set scale, click both ends of something you know (a door width, a table edge) and type its real length. This step is optional and can be done at any time later. (Printed markers and tape measurements from the Phase 12 capture pack remain available for research experiments but are not required by the Studio.)

6. Privacy

Remove or cover personal documents, screens and family photos before capturing. Everything is processed on your own computer; nothing is uploaded to the internet unless you publish the walkthrough yourself.
